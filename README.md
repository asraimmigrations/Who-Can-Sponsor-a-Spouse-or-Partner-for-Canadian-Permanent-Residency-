# Who Can Sponsor a Spouse or Partner for Canadian Permanent Residency?

Building a life together is an important goal for many couples, especially when one partner lives in Canada and the other lives abroad. Whether you are married, living together in a long-term relationship, or facing circumstances that prevent you from living in the same country, Canada's family sponsorship program may offer a pathway to permanent residency.

For French-speaking couples in Quebec and across Canada, understanding the sponsorship process is particularly important. Although the basic federal eligibility rules apply throughout much of the country, Quebec has additional requirements that sponsors must consider.

The good news is that Canadian citizens and permanent residents may be eligible to sponsor a spouse or qualifying partner for permanent residency if they meet the applicable conditions. However, not every relationship qualifies, and sponsors must demonstrate that their relationship is genuine and that they can meet their sponsorship responsibilities.

## What Is Spousal Sponsorship in Canada?

Spousal sponsorship is a Canadian immigration program that allows eligible Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor their spouse, common-law partner, or conjugal partner for permanent residency.

The purpose of the program is to help eligible couples build their lives together in Canada. It recognizes that family unity is an important part of immigration and provides a legal pathway for qualifying partners to apply for permanent resident status.

There are two main parts to the process. The sponsor applies to sponsor their partner, while the partner being sponsored applies for permanent residency. Both applications must satisfy the applicable requirements, and the relationship must meet Canada's immigration rules.

Spousal sponsorship is available to couples of different genders and same-gender couples. The important considerations include the sponsor's eligibility, the nature of the relationship, the supporting documents, and the applicant's admissibility to Canada.

An application is not automatically approved simply because a couple is married. Immigration officers assess the evidence and determine whether the relationship is genuine and whether the applicants meet the legal requirements.
## Who Can Sponsor a Spouse or Partner for Canadian Permanent Residency?

To sponsor a spouse or partner, you must meet specific eligibility requirements established by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

In general, you can sponsor your partner if you meet the following conditions:

- You are at least 18 years old.
- You are a Canadian citizen, a Canadian permanent resident, or a person registered in Canada under the Canadian Indian Act.
- You live in Canada, subject to the exception for Canadian citizens living abroad.
- You agree to provide the required financial support and sign the sponsorship undertaking.
- You meet the other applicable legal requirements.

For example, a Canadian citizen living in Montreal may be eligible to sponsor a spouse living in France. A permanent resident living in Toronto may also be eligible to sponsor a spouse living abroad, provided they meet the requirements.

However, a permanent resident who lives outside Canada cannot sponsor a spouse under this program. A Canadian citizen living abroad may sponsor an eligible spouse or partner if they demonstrate that they intend to live in Canada when the sponsored person becomes a permanent resident.

Meeting these basic conditions is the first step. You must also establish that your relationship falls within an eligible category.

## Which Relationships Qualify for Spousal Sponsorship?

Canada recognizes three main categories of relationships for spouse or partner sponsorship: legally married spouses, common-law partners, and conjugal partners.

Understanding the differences is essential because each category has its own eligibility requirements and supporting documents.

### 1. Legally married spouses

If you are legally married, you may be eligible to sponsor your spouse for Canadian permanent residency.

Your marriage must be legally valid under the applicable rules. If you married outside Canada, the marriage generally must be legally valid in the country where it took place and recognized under Canadian law.

Both partners must be at least 18 years old, and the relationship must be genuine rather than entered into primarily to obtain immigration status.

For example, a Canadian citizen living in Quebec who married their partner in France may be eligible to sponsor them, provided the marriage meets the relevant legal requirements and the couple satisfies the other sponsorship conditions.

A marriage certificate is important evidence, but it may not be sufficient on its own. Immigration officers may also examine the couple's communication history, shared financial responsibilities, photographs, travel records, and other evidence demonstrating that the relationship is genuine.

Canada does not recognize marriages conducted by proxy, telephone, fax, or internet when one or both partners were not physically present at the ceremony. Couples who married through one of these arrangements should seek guidance on whether their relationship qualifies under another category

### 2. Common-law partners

You do not necessarily need to be legally married to qualify for partner sponsorship.

Canada recognizes common-law relationships when a couple has lived together in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 consecutive months. Short, temporary periods apart may be acceptable, but extended separations can affect whether the requirement is met.

A common-law relationship involves more than simply sharing an address. The couple should be able to demonstrate a genuine, committed relationship and a shared life.

Useful supporting documents may include a lease or property records showing a shared residence, joint bank statements, utility bills, insurance documents, and official correspondence sent to both partners at the same address.

Not every couple will have joint financial accounts, particularly if they maintain separate finances. In that situation, other reliable evidence can help demonstrate cohabitation and the nature of the relationship.

For instance, a French-speaking couple who have lived together continuously for 14 months may qualify as common-law partners even if they have not held a wedding ceremony.

The key is to provide credible evidence that the relationship meets the legal definition.

### 3. Conjugal partners

A conjugal partner is someone with whom you have had a genuine, committed relationship for at least one year, but who does not qualify as your spouse or common-law partner.

This category is intended for particular circumstances in which the couple has been unable to marry or live together because of significant barriers, such as legal, immigration, social, cultural, or religious restrictions.

The sponsored partner must live outside Canada. The relationship must also involve substantial emotional, social, and financial interdependence rather than simply dating or maintaining a long-distance relationship.

For example, a couple may have maintained a committed relationship for several years but face legal or other serious obstacles that prevent them from marrying or living together. Depending on the circumstances, conjugal partner sponsorship may be an option.

However, being in a long-distance relationship does not automatically qualify you for this category. Couples who could marry or establish common-law status but have chosen not to do so may not meet the requirements.

Because this category has specific eligibility conditions, it is particularly important to understand the rules before submitting an application.
## Can a Permanent Resident Sponsor a Spouse Who Lives Abroad?

Yes. A Canadian permanent resident may generally sponsor an eligible spouse or partner who lives outside Canada, provided the sponsor lives in Canada and meets the other requirements.

The application process is commonly referred to as outland sponsorship when the sponsored partner lives abroad or the application is made through the Family Class.

For example, a permanent resident living in Ottawa may sponsor a spouse who currently lives in France. The spouse can apply for permanent residency through the family sponsorship process while the couple remains in different countries during processing.

The application must include the required sponsorship forms, the permanent residence application, evidence of the relationship, and supporting documents relevant to the applicants' circumstances.

Processing times vary according to the application category, country-specific requirements, completeness of the application, and other factors. Couples should consult IRCC's current processing-time information rather than relying on a fixed timeline.

Official resource: [IRCC spouse and partner sponsorship program](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-children).

## Is There a Minimum Income Requirement to Sponsor a Spouse?

One of the most common questions concerns the sponsor's income.

In most cases, there is no minimum income requirement for sponsoring a spouse, common-law partner, or conjugal partner under the federal program.

However, the sponsor must still agree to provide the required support and demonstrate that they meet the applicable conditions. Certain exceptions apply, particularly when the sponsored partner has a dependent child who also has a dependent child of their own.

This means that having a modest income does not automatically prevent someone from sponsoring a spouse. Nevertheless, sponsors should carefully consider their financial responsibilities before applying.

If you live in Quebec, additional provincial rules apply. Quebec has its own sponsorship and undertaking requirements, including income requirements in applicable cases. Do not assume that the general federal rule applies without modification to your Quebec application.

## Who Cannot Sponsor a Spouse or Partner?

Even if you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, certain circumstances can make you ineligible to sponsor your spouse.

You may be unable to sponsor your partner if:

- You became a permanent resident after being sponsored by a spouse or partner less than five years ago.
- You previously sponsored a spouse or partner, and fewer than three years have passed since they became a permanent resident.
- You receive social assistance for a reason other than a disability.
- You have certain outstanding immigration debts or court-ordered family support obligations.
- You have not fulfilled a previous sponsorship undertaking.
- You are in prison or have certain criminal convictions that affect eligibility.
- You are subject to a removal order or have other legal circumstances that prevent sponsorship.

These restrictions do not apply identically in every situation, and certain Quebec-specific exceptions exist. The nature of the conviction, the applicable legal provisions, and the sponsor's circumstances may affect eligibility.


Before applying, review the official eligibility criteria carefully. If you have previously sponsored a family member, received social assistance, or had a criminal or immigration matter, consider obtaining qualified advice about your particular circumstances.

## What Documents Are Needed for Spouse or Partner Sponsorship?

The documents required depend on the relationship category, the country where the sponsored partner lives, and the details of the application.

Although every couple should follow their personalized IRCC checklist, the following documents are commonly relevant.

Identity and civil status documents

Passports, birth certificates where requested, marriage certificates, and relevant divorce or separation documents from previous relationships.

Evidence of a genuine relationship

Photographs, travel records, communication history, shared financial documents, or other evidence appropriate to your relationship category.

Sponsorship and permanent residence forms

The required forms, declarations, and supporting documents listed in the current application package.

Police certificates, medical examinations, and biometrics

These may be required as part of the sponsored person's admissibility assessment, according to the applicable instructions.

Couples should also pay attention to translations. If a document is in French, English, or another language, follow the specific translation instructions in the application guide. Do not assume that every document in French needs translation simply because the application is being processed federally.

French-speaking applicants should also ensure that names, dates, addresses, and relationship information are consistent across all forms and supporting documents.

A complete application is important because missing documents or inconsistent information can lead to delays or rejection of an incomplete submission.

## How Does the Spousal Sponsorship Application Process Work?

The process generally involves several steps, from confirming eligibility to receiving a final decision.

First, the sponsor should verify that they meet the eligibility conditions and that the relationship qualifies under the relevant category.

Next, both partners should gather the required documents. This includes evidence of the relationship, identity documents, completed forms, and any country-specific requirements. It is important to declare all required family members, including dependent children who are not accompanying the applicant, as instructed by IRCC.

The couple then submits the sponsorship and permanent residence applications together through the appropriate online process and pays the applicable fees.

After submission, IRCC reviews the application and may request additional documents, biometrics, medical examinations, or further information. The sponsored partner must meet the applicable admissibility requirements.

If the application is approved, the sponsored partner can complete the required steps to become a Canadian permanent resident.

For couples living in Quebec, an additional provincial undertaking process applies. IRCC instructs applicants when to submit the undertaking to Quebec, and couples should wait for that instruction before filing it. Quebec also has specific sponsorship rules and processing arrangements that may affect the process.

You can find the official instructions here: [How to apply for spouse or partner sponsorship](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-children/).

## What Should French-Speaking Couples in Quebec Know?

Quebec has a distinct immigration framework in several areas, including the sponsorship of spouses and partners.

If you live in Montreal, Quebec City, Gatineau, Sherbrooke, or another Quebec community, you must consider both the applicable federal rules and Quebec's provincial requirements.

The process may include signing an undertaking with the Government of Quebec, meeting relevant provincial conditions, and following instructions about when to submit the provincial application.

Quebec's requirements may differ from those in Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, and other provinces. Couples should therefore avoid relying exclusively on advice written for applicants living elsewhere in Canada.

Language can also be an important practical consideration. French-speaking applicants may find it easier to review official guidance in French and prepare documents using the language options available through the relevant government services.

For more information, visit the [Government of Quebec immigration website](https://www.quebec.ca/en/immigration) and review the requirements applicable to your circumstances.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Sponsoring a Spouse

One of the most common mistakes is assuming that marriage alone guarantees eligibility. Although a legally valid marriage may establish the relationship category, the couple must still meet the sponsorship requirements and provide evidence of a genuine relationship.

Another mistake is submitting insufficient evidence. Couples should organize documents that demonstrate their relationship history and shared life, where relevant, rather than relying on a single certificate or a small collection of photographs.

Some applicants also overlook previous relationships, dependent children, or the sponsor's past immigration history. These details may affect eligibility and must be disclosed accurately as required.

Quebec applicants sometimes make the mistake of treating the provincial undertaking as identical to the federal sponsorship application. Both processes must be completed according to the applicable instructions and sequence.

Finally, avoid submitting false statements or fabricated relationship evidence. Misrepresentation can have serious consequences for both partners.

Careful preparation, honest disclosure, and attention to official instructions can help couples avoid preventable problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can a Canadian citizen sponsor a spouse who lives outside Canada?

Yes. A Canadian citizen may generally sponsor an eligible spouse or partner living abroad. If the sponsor also lives abroad, they must demonstrate that they intend to live in Canada when their partner becomes a permanent resident.

### 2. Can a permanent resident sponsor a spouse?

Yes, provided the permanent resident lives in Canada and meets the other applicable requirements. Permanent residents living outside Canada cannot sponsor a spouse under this program.

### 3. Do I need a high income to sponsor my spouse in Canada?

In most cases, there is no minimum income requirement for sponsoring a spouse or partner under the federal program. Certain exceptions apply, and Quebec has additional requirements in applicable cases.

### 4. Can I sponsor my partner if we are not married?

Yes. You may qualify if you meet the requirements for a common-law partner or, in limited circumstances, a conjugal partner. Common-law partners generally must have lived together in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 consecutive months.

### 5. Can I sponsor my spouse if I receive social assistance?

Receiving social assistance for a reason other than a disability can make you ineligible to sponsor. The rules contain certain exceptions, including specific provisions for Quebec, so review your circumstances carefully.

### 6. How long does spousal sponsorship take in Canada?

Processing times vary depending on the application category, where the sponsored partner lives, the completeness of the application, and other factors. Check IRCC's current processing-time information for the latest estimate.

### 7. Do I need an immigration consultant to sponsor my spouse?

No. You can apply directly through the official IRCC process. If you choose to pay for professional assistance, make sure the representative is authorized to provide Canadian immigration advice.
